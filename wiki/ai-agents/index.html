Use Graft from an AI Agent

Graft is fully scriptable: every action in the app is a graft command, and almost every command can answer in JSON. So an AI coding agent that runs Terminal commands on your Mac, such as Claude Code, can drive Graft for you from a request in plain English.

What it can do

An agent can take the same steps you would, in order:

  • search the App Store and add an app;
  • get the app's IPA from the App Store, or stage an IPA you give it;
  • search Canister or your repos for tweaks, download them and attach them;
  • build the app and deliver it to your iPhone or iPad;
  • check what's out of date, what was last delivered, and why a build failed.

It reads Graft's results and its hints as it goes, so it can tell you what went wrong and what to do next. graft --describe gives it the full list of commands, flags and events.

Set it up

  1. Set up Graft.app first: its first run, and for App Store downloads, Sign in and Set up decryption.
  2. In Graft.app, open Settings › Command-line tool and press Install graft for Terminal (see Installing the CLI). In a new Terminal window, graft --version prints Graft's version.
  3. Make a folder for the agent to work in, and save AGENTS.md from Graft's repository into it. It tells an agent how to call graft, how to read its answers, and what to leave to you. Some agents read a file with that name by themselves; with others, start by asking the agent to read it.
  4. Open your agent in that folder.

The agent runs graft as you, with your config, apps and settings, just as if you typed the commands yourself.

Example requests

  • "Add Example App from the App Store, attach the tweak com.example.sometweak from Canister, and install it to my iPhone through LiveContainer."
  • "Search Canister for tweaks for Example App, show me what you find, and attach the one I pick."
  • "Check whether any of my apps has a newer version on the App Store, or any of my tweaks a newer release, and tell me what you'd rebuild."
  • "My last build of Example App failed. Find out why and tell me what to do."

What it does, and what it leaves to you

AGENTS.md lets an agent search, list and check things, add an app, download an app your Apple ID already has, fetch and attach tweaks, and preview a build with --dry-run, without asking.

It tells the agent to ask you first before:

  • anything that changes your iPhone or iPad: a build that delivers to it, installing or cleaning up LiveContainer, or a re-install;
  • getting a free app your Apple ID doesn't have yet. Graft never buys anything: the agent asks before it adds a free app to your Apple ID's purchases, and a paid app is yours to buy in the App Store;
  • removing anything: an app, a tweak, a staged IPA or old builds.

And it tells the agent never to:

  • sign in to the App Store for you, or ask for your Apple ID password or the code Apple sends. You sign in in Graft.app;
  • set up decryption, which needs your administrator password in macOS's own dialog. You press Set Up Decryption… yourself;
  • ask for or type any other password: your Mac's, your .p12's or your device's SSH password;
  • answer a keychain or password dialog for you. If one appears while the agent works, it says which command caused it.

An agent can still get things wrong. Read what it's about to do to your device before you say yes.

LiveContainer delivery puts the IPA in LiveContainer's Documents on your device. Opening LiveContainer and importing it is still your step, as it is when you build from the app.

Graft is for personal use, whoever runs it: your own Apple ID, apps you own or get for free, on your own devices. Don't redistribute decrypted or modified IPAs.

Privacy

The agent runs on your Mac and sees what Graft prints: your apps and tweaks, your devices' names and UDIDs, file paths in your home folder, and, from graft --appstore-status, the email of the Apple ID you signed in with. Your passwords stay in your login keychain and aren't part of that output.

If the agent's AI model runs online, what the agent reads is sent to that service, and that service's terms apply to it.
